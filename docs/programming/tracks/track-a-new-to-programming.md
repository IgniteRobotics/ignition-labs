# Track A — New to Programming

**This track is for you if:** you have never written code, or you have only done a little in a class or a workshop and it has not stuck yet.

You are the reason this curriculum exists. Everything here assumes you are starting from zero, and nothing in it assumes you have seen a curly brace before.

---

## What you are signing up for

You walk the whole ladder. Ten milestones, M0 through M9.

That sounds like a lot. It is spread across a year, most of it is hands-on rather than reading, and by the end you will have written code that drives a real robot at a real competition.

**Realistic timeline:** M0 through M3 takes most of a fall — call it 15 to 20 sessions. That block is what you need before kickoff. Everything after that runs at your own pace.

## Four things worth knowing up front

**You will not use AI to write code until M7.** That is going to feel unfair, especially when you know these tools exist and work. The reasoning is in `../ai-use-standard.md` §4 and it is worth reading rather than taking on faith. The short version: research tracking students exactly like you found that the ones who leaned on AI early ended up believing they understood things they did not, and it caught up with them later. The struggle in M1 and M2 is measured in hours. Take the hours.

**Getting stuck is the job, not a sign you are bad at this.** Everyone stares at a compiler error for fifteen minutes. The difference between people who get good at this and people who quit is entirely about what happens at minute sixteen. At minute sixteen, ask someone.

**Ask humans.** You have mentors and you have other students. Using them is not cheating and it is not weakness — it is the single fastest way to learn this, and it is better than any tool. The most-endorsed advice in the FRC community on this is blunt: experienced humans will beat an LLM every time.

**Everything runs in simulation.** You do not need the robot. You do not need to wait your turn. You can do every project on your own laptop at home, and you cannot break anything.

## How to know you are done with a milestone

Every milestone has a **Definition of done** — a checklist where every item is a yes or no, not a judgment call. Work through it with a mentor. When every box is checked, you are done, and it does not matter how long it took.

Nobody is comparing your pace to anyone else's.

---

## What you are aiming at

By the end of M3 you can open `Robot-2026/src/main/java/frc/robot/RobotContainer.java` — the file the whole robot is wired up in — and understand what it does.

Open it now. It will look like nonsense. That is the point; keep it around and open it again after M3.
